For getting started in primary school, special schools and lower secondary, the worlds of the introductory modules (E1–E3) can be rebuilt with a 3D printer. Children first lay out a task on the table, guide the character by hand and call out the commands. Then they solve the same task on the computer. This takes them through the three levels of representation that many curricula name: **enactive** (acting), **iconic** (picture of the world, blocks) and **symbolic** (Klonk-Script).

All parts have the dimensions of the Klonk world: **1 cell = 4 × 4 studs = 32 mm**, a brick is 9.6 mm high, a plate 3.2 mm. They therefore fit common interlocking bricks; bricks the school already has can be used too.

The character has a face with a nose: **the nose shows the viewing direction.** This matters because `step`, `turnLeft` and `placeBrick` depend on the viewing direction.

## Connecting plates

Each plate has three tabs on its **right and back edges** and three matching sockets on its **left and front edges**. The dovetails sit in the lower half of the plate, so all studs on top stay free. To connect, place the second plate **from above** onto the tabs of the first and press down.

- Two plates side by side give a world of 6 × 3 cells (task *Continue the pattern* in E3).
- Four plates give 6 × 6 cells, six plates 9 × 6 cells – suitable for your own tasks from the [world editor](/en/world/editor).
- So that the cells on the table have the same coordinates as in Klonk, cell 1|1 is at the back left (north is at the top of the screen).

## How much to print?

For one group that lays out all introductory tasks on the table:

| Part | Number per group | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Base plate 3 × 3 | 2 | grey or green |
| Brick | 6 red, 1 each green and yellow | as in the program |
| Mark | 5 yellow, 3 red, 3 blue | mark colours (only yellow, red, blue, green) |
| Block | 1 | grey |
| Character | 1 | any |

## Print settings

- **Material:** PLA or PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height (character preferably 0.12–0.16 mm).
- **Orientation:** plates, bricks, marks and blocks with the open underside down; the character standing. **No supports** are needed: the head and arms are chamfered underneath.
- **Infill:** 15–20 %; the character with 3 walls and 100 % infill so that it stands firmly.
- **Fit:** the templates use the usual nominal sizes (stud 4.8 mm, tube inside 4.8 mm, outside 6.51 mm, gap 0.1 mm per side). Printers differ: if parts are too tight, reduce `sd` (stud diameter) in the design by 0.05–0.1 mm; if too loose, increase it. Test one brick and one plate first.
- **Plate:** if the print bed is smaller than 110 × 110 mm, the plate does not fit; the tabs protrude 5 mm.

## Adapting the design

The parts are designed parametrically in SolidCraft; the source files are in the repository under `assets/print/`:

| File | Parameters |
|---|---|
| `klonk-ziegel.solidcraft` | `P` stud pitch (8 mm), `gap` gap per side, `sd`/`sh` stud, `h` height – **9.6 mm = brick, 3.2 mm = mark, 28.8 mm = block** |
| `klonk-platte-3x3.solidcraft` | `P`, `gap`, `sd`, `sh`, `t` plate thickness, `j` height of the dovetails |
| `klonk-figur.solidcraft` | fixed dimensions; stud holes 4.9 mm |

## In class

1. **Role play:** one child is Klonk, the class gives the commands (`step`, `turnLeft`, `turnRight`).
2. **At the table:** the task is built on the plate. One child guides the character, another calls out the commands – or lays command cards in a row.
3. **At the computer:** solve the same task in the learning module with blocks and have it checked.
4. **Compare:** does the result on the screen match the setup on the table? The 3D view can be rotated to match the view of the table exactly.

For children who still struggle with the mouse or with words, the step at the table can remain the focus for a longer time – the program on the computer is added once the command sequences are secure.
