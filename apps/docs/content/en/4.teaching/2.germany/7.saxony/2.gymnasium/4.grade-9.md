## Elective area 3 Graphs

*Visualise computer science problems with graphs: neural networks, shortest path according to Dijkstra, travelling salesman problem, modelling states, Huffman coding*

A Klonk world is a **grid graph**: every free cell is a node, and neighbouring cells without a block between them are connected by edges. This makes two of the examples mentioned tangible without new programming knowledge:

- **Shortest path:** Build a small maze in the [world editor](/en/world/editor), transfer it to paper as a graph and carry out Dijkstra by hand. Piles of bricks stand for “expensive” cells (edge weight = number of bricks). Then the learners steer the character along the calculated path with a short program and mark it.
- **Modelling states:** Extend the transition graph of the viewing direction from grade 7 – e.g. design a program “follow the wall” as a state diagram and then implement it.

The learners only program the algorithm itself in upper secondary; in grade 9 it remains a matter of visualising and following it.

## And the compulsory learning areas?

Klonk does not cover databases, machine learning and network technology – there are more suitable tools for these (database system, AI learning environments, network simulation). If you have time for revision before grade 10, use the [tasks from grade 8](/en/teaching/germany/saxony/gymnasium/grade-8) in [Klonk-Script](/en/klonk-script/introduction).
