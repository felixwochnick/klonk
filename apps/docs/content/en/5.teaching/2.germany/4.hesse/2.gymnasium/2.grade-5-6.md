## Basics of programming (grade 5)

*Recognizing algorithms in everyday life, formulating them in pseudocode; building blocks statement, sequence, condition, repetition; implementing them in an age-appropriate (e.g. block-based) language; discussing the influence of programrs on algorithms*

The teaching guide suggests Scratch, Blockly or code.org. Klonk works with **Blockly** – the [blocks](/en/klonk-script/blocks) are labeled in German or English and, instead of a cat, control a character in a 3D world of interlocking bricks.

### Lesson sequence

| Step | Building block | Task in Klonk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Statement, sequence | Lead the character to a marked cell; lay out the path as pseudocode on cards first |
| 2 | Repetition with a count | Wall of four bricks, walk around a square (`repeat 4 times`) |
| 3 | Repetition with a condition | Walk to the wall – in worlds of different sizes |
| 4 | Condition | Place a brick only on marked cells |
| 5 | Everything together | "Lawn mower robot": cover a whole area row by row and mark it |

```klonk
// Walk to the wall, mark every cell, build on marked cells
repeat while not isWall
  if isMark then
    turnLeft
    placeBrick(green)
    turnRight
  else
    setMark(yellow)
  end
  step
end
```

### Tips

- **From pseudocode to blocks:** The learners first write their process as pseudocode or a structogram (the "DW booklet" of the teaching guide works with Nassi–Shneiderman blocks), then they arrange the blocks. With **Show Klonk-Script**, they see that blocks turn into text.
- **Make the process visible:** The block currently running lights up; with single step and the speed slider, the class follows every step.
- **Lawn mower start:** The teaching guide begins with a robotic lawn mower. Exactly that can be rebuilt in Klonk: marks = mowed grass, blocks = flower bed that must be driven around.
- **Who decides?** Two groups solve the same task differently (e.g. going left or right around an obstacle). This can be used to discuss the *influence of programrs*.

## Computers everywhere (grade 5)

*Hardware/software, IPO model (input – processing – output), sensors and actuators, robots*

Klonk characters are suitable as a **model** of a robot: `isWall`, `isBrick` and `isMark` are sensors, `step` and `placeBrick` actuators. The learners classify the commands into input – processing – output. This does not replace real hardware.

## Microcontrollers, encryption, internet (grade 6)

According to the teaching guide, the programming part in grade 6 uses **microcontrollers** (Calliope, micro:bit, Makey Makey) with real sensors and actuators – Klonk cannot do that. At most, Klonk can be used beforehand to revise the control structures. Klonk does not cover encryption, data protection and the HTML website.
