At Bavarian special schools (*Förderschulen*, also *Förderzentren*), **Computer Science is a compulsory subject from grade 5** – just as at the Mittelschule. The LehrplanPLUS Förderschule contains separate subject curricula for each special educational focus (*Förderschwerpunkt*) that add **development-related competences** (*entwicklungsbezogene Kompetenzen*: motor skills and perception, thinking and learning strategies, communication and language, emotions and social behaviour) to the learning areas of the Mittelschule. Each of these subject curricula contains a learning area **Programming** (*Programmieren*); this is where Klonk can be used.

**Primary level (grades 1–4):** there is no subject Computer Science; the curriculum of the [Grundschule](/en/teaching/germany/bavaria/grundschule/overview) applies together with the development-related competences of the respective focus. According to the subject profile Computer Science (focus on learning), children there develop media skills "mainly through play" in line with the school's media concept. The exception is the focus on vision with the subject *Information and Communication Technology Education* (*Informations- und kommunikationstechnische Bildung*, IKB) in grades 2 to 4 – touch typing and aids such as screen readers, no programming.

## Why Klonk fits special schools

The subject curricula describe the start of programming explicitly in the way Klonk works:

- **Role play "human robot"** (*Menschroboter*) as an introduction (focus on learning, 4.2) – in Klonk, students then control a character with the same commands.
- **"Programming blocks"** as a given aid (learning, 4.1.2) – Klonk offers [blocks](/en/klonk-script/blocks) in German, without typing errors.
- **All levels of representation** – enactive, iconic, symbolic (subject profile): playing the robot yourself, laying blocks, later reading text in [Klonk-Script](/en/klonk-script/introduction).
- **"Sequences of steps when moving a figure"** (grade 5, focuses with Mittelschule learning areas) – the Robot Karol principle.
- **Dealing constructively with mistakes** (learning, 4.1.4) – the character immediately shows where the program is wrong; the mistake is visible but not exposing.

## Notes on differentiation

- **Pace:** with the speed slider and **single step** (F10), each command runs on its own and the active block is highlighted. This supports students with difficulties in attention and action planning.
- **Small-step worlds:** with the [world editor](/en/world/editor), the teacher prepares worlds at several levels of difficulty (few cells, one mark as a goal – up to obstacles).
- **Differentiated tasks via the cloud:** in the [Klonk Cloud](/en/klonk-script/cloud), the teacher creates assignments for the group and sees the submissions. The [learning modules](/en/teaching/modules/overview) check solutions automatically – the task texts are written for the Mittelschule and should be read aloud or linguistically prepared for the focus on learning.
- **The same environment over years:** from the first block in grade 5 to variables in grade 9, the interface stays the same – important for students who find changes difficult.
- **No installation:** Klonk runs in the browser. Programs can be created as blocks with the mouse or as Klonk-Script with the keyboard – whichever input is easier for a student. Operation with assistive input devices has not yet been tested separately.

## Intellectual development

For the focus on intellectual development, neither the timetable nor the LehrplanPLUS provides a **subject Computer Science**; working with digital media is anchored across subjects. Klonk is not intended as a regular teaching tool here. In individual cases – for example for students who can already reliably break processes down into steps – the character can serve as a motivating cause-and-effect game with two or three blocks (`schritt`, `linksDrehen`, `markieren`). Klonk: ★.

## For school leadership and school administration

Special schools with a lower secondary level (*Mittelschulstufe*) teach Computer Science according to the timetable of the *Volksschulordnung-F* with one lesson per week in each grade, plus the elective *Computer Science and Digital Design*. Klonk covers the learning area *Programming*, not the whole subject (hardware, networks, word processing, spreadsheets, presentation and touch typing require other tools). Questions about operation, data protection and licensing are covered in [For schools and school administration](/en/teaching/schools).
