In Germany, each state (*Bundesland*) sets its own curricula. Computer science content is therefore found in subjects with different names – sometimes as a subject of its own, *Informatik* (computer science), sometimes as part of a combined subject such as *Natur und Technik* (science and technology). With its strategy "Education in the digital world" (*Bildung in der digitalen Welt*), the Standing Conference of the Ministers of Education (*Kultusministerkonferenz*) provides a common framework that also includes understanding algorithms.

## What is the same everywhere

Regardless of the state, the following topics appear in almost every curriculum; Klonk is designed for them:

- **Concept of an algorithm:** describing processes as a sequence of unambiguous instructions
- **Control structures:** sequence, conditional statement, repetition with a fixed count and with a condition
- **Structuring:** own procedures, functions with parameters and return values, variables
- **Object orientation:** objects, classes, attributes and methods
- **Testing and debugging:** running programs step by step, setting breakpoints, finding errors
- **Forms of representation:** structogram, UML class and object diagram, ER diagram – Klonk generates them from the program or database ([Diagrams](/en/klonk-script/diagrams)), so students can compare their own designs with them
