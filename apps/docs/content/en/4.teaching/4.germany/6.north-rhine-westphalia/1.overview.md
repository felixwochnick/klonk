In North Rhine-Westphalia, the **core curricula** (*Kernlehrpläne*) of the Ministry of Schools and Education (*Ministerium für Schule und Bildung*) apply. They are published by the Quality and Support Agency – State Institute for Schools (*Qualitäts- und UnterstützungsAgentur – Landesinstitut für Schule*, QUA-LiS NRW) in the [Lehrplannavigator](https://lehrplannavigator.nrw.de) (curriculum navigator), together with examples of school-internal curricula. Each core curriculum divides a subject into **competence areas** (*Kompetenzbereiche*: reasoning, modelling and implementing, representing and interpreting, communicating and cooperating) and **content fields** (*Inhaltsfelder*) with content focuses. The core curricula do not specify hours per content field; each school sets the distribution in its **school-internal curriculum** (*schulinterner Lehrplan*).

## Computer science in NRW

- **Compulsory computer science in grades 5/6:** Since the 2021/22 school year, computer science has been a compulsory subject at all secondary school types (Gymnasium, *Gesamtschule*, *Realschule*, *Hauptschule* and *Sekundarschule*). A shared core curriculum applies; some competence expectations are compulsory only at the Gymnasium.
- **Computer science as an elective subject:** In the middle grades, computer science can be chosen as an elective subject (*Wahlpflichtfach*) – as a stand-alone subject or in a combination of subjects.
- **Upper secondary (*gymnasiale Oberstufe*):** Computer science as a basic or advanced course, with a centralised Abitur (*Zentralabitur*) in Java and SQL. A new core curriculum will be introduced from the 2027/28 school year.

## Media competence framework NRW

In addition to the subject curricula, the [media competence framework NRW](https://medienkompetenzrahmen.nrw) (*Medienkompetenzrahmen NRW*) applies to all subjects. Its competence area **6 “Problem solving and modelling”** (*Problemlösen und Modellieren*; including 6.3 “Modelling and programming”) is covered mainly in computer science. Klonk can be assigned to this competence area.

## Klonk in NRW

Many schools use Robot Karol, Kara or a block-based environment to introduce algorithms. Klonk follows the same principle – a character that places bricks, sets marks and detects walls – and can be used across the grades without changing tools:

- [blocks](/en/klonk-script/blocks) for the “visual programming language” required by the core curriculum in grades 5/6,
- [Python](/en/klonk-script/python) for the elective subject,
- [Java](/en/klonk-script/java) for upper secondary – the language of the centralised Abitur,
- in the browser, without installation, with a debugger in all languages.

## For school leadership and administration

With computer science compulsory in grades 5/6 since 2021/22 and the introduction of the new core curriculum for upper secondary from 1 August 2027 (phased in from the EF), schools face the question of a programming environment that is viable from the orientation stage through to the centralised Abitur. Klonk covers the visual programming language of grades 5/6 as well as Java for upper secondary and runs in the browser without installation. Information on operation, data protection, sign-in and licensing is provided on the page [For schools and school administration](/en/teaching/schools).

For the content area Algorithms in grades 5/6 and in the elective subject in grades 9 and 10, [learning modules](/en/teaching/modules/overview) with automatically checked tasks are also available. Teachers enable them for their group and see the learning progress of the group; the work is stored in Klonk Cloud. The grade pages indicate which modules fit.
