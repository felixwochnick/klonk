## Overview

**As of November 2026:** *Digital World* has been piloted since 2022/23 (most recently at around 80 schools) and continues from 2026/27 as an [all-day offer](https://kultus.hessen.de/ab-20262027-an-allen-ganztagsschulen-der-sekundarstufe-i). Building on this, [*AI and Digital World*](https://kultus.hessen.de/digitale-zukunftskompetenzen) becomes a compulsory subject – one lesson per week or in blocks, with media education, basic computer science education, AI, and economic, financial and consumer education. The core curriculum for it has not been published yet. The table therefore follows the **teaching guide for the pilot program** (*Handreichung zum Schulversuch*; two lessons per week, 56 lesson units (UE) per school year; the UE figures are marked there as *exemplary* teaching and learning goals).

Klonk is suited above all to the topic area *Basics of programming* in grade 5. The programming part in grade 6 relies on microcontrollers and cannot be implemented with Klonk.

## Basics of programming (grade 5)

**Reference to the teaching guide (short version):** *Recognizing algorithms in everyday life, formulating them in pseudocode; building blocks statement, sequence, condition, repetition; implementing them in an age-appropriate (e.g. block-based) language; discussing the influence of programmers on algorithms*

The teaching guide suggests Scratch, Blockly or code.org. Klonk works with **Blockly**: the [blocks](/en/klonk-script/blocks) are labeled in German (or English) and, instead of a cat, control a character in a 3D world of interlocking bricks.

### Lesson sequence

| Step | Building block | Task in Klonk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Statement, sequence | Lead the character to a marked cell; lay out the path as pseudocode on cards first |
| 2 | Repetition with a count | Wall of four bricks, walk around a square (`repeat 4 times`) |
| 3 | Repetition with a condition | Walk to the wall – in worlds of different sizes |
| 4 | Condition | Place a brick only on marked cells |
| 5 | Everything together | "Lawn mower robot": cover a whole area row by row and mark it |

```klonk
// Walk to the wall, mark every cell, build on marked cells
repeat while not isWall
  if isMark then
    turnLeft
    placeBrick(green)
    turnRight
  else
    setMark(yellow)
  end
  step
end
```

## Computers everywhere (grade 5)

**Reference to the teaching guide (short version):** *Hardware/software, IPO model (input – processing – output), sensors and actuators, robots*

Klonk characters can be used as a **model** of a robot: `isWall`, `isBrick` and `isMark` are sensors, `step` and `placeBrick` actuators. Students classify the commands into input – processing – output. This does not replace real hardware.

## Microcontrollers, encryption, internet (grade 6)

According to the teaching guide, the programming part in grade 6 uses **microcontrollers** (Calliope, micro:bit, Makey Makey) with real sensors and actuators; Klonk does not provide this. At most, Klonk can be used beforehand to revise the control structures. Klonk does not cover encryption, data protection and the HTML website.

## Matching learning modules

The [learning modules](/en/teaching/modules/overview) contain tasks in blocks or Klonk-Script with automatic checking that students can work on independently – in class or at home. The following modules fit "Fundamentals of programming" in grade 5:

- [First steps: instructions](/en/teaching/modules/sequence) – instruction and sequence
- [Repetition with a fixed count](/en/teaching/modules/repeat) – repetition with a count
- [Conditional repetition](/en/teaching/modules/while) – repetition with a condition
- [Branching](/en/teaching/modules/conditions) – condition

In grade 6, the same modules can serve to revise the control structures before working with microcontrollers.

## Notes for lesson planning

- **From pseudocode to blocks:** Students first formulate their process as pseudocode or a structogram (the "DW booklet" of the teaching guide works with Nassi–Shneiderman blocks) and then implement it in blocks. **Show Klonk-Script** makes visible that blocks turn into text.
- **Make the process visible:** The block currently being executed is highlighted; with single step and the speed slider, the class can follow every step.
- **Build on the lawn mower introduction:** The teaching guide begins with a robotic lawn mower. This can be reproduced in Klonk: marks = mowed grass, blocks = flower bed that must be driven around. Suitable worlds can be prepared in the [world editor](/en/world/editor) and distributed as files.
- **Influence of programmers:** Two groups solve the same task in different ways (e.g. going left or right around an obstacle). This can be used to discuss the *influence of programmers* named in the teaching guide.
