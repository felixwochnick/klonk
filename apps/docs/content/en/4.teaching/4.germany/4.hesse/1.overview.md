In Hesse, the curricula are called **core curricula** (*Kerncurricula*, KC). They are issued by the Hessian Ministry for Culture, Education and Opportunities (*Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen*) and published at [kultus.hessen.de → Kerncurricula](https://kultus.hessen.de/unterricht/kerncurricula-und-lehrplaene/kerncurricula). A core curriculum describes **educational standards** (*Bildungsstandards* – what students can do at the end of a learning period) and arranges the binding content into **topic areas** (*Themenfelder*) with **content focuses** (*inhaltliche Schwerpunkte*). The computer science core curricula do not specify hours per topic area; for the upper secondary level there are guideline values in teaching weeks.

There are separate core curricula for each school type in lower secondary (*Sekundarstufe I*), and for the upper secondary level the **core curriculum for the upper secondary level of the Gymnasium** (*Kerncurriculum gymnasiale Oberstufe*, KCGO). The computer science core curricula are based on the educational standards of the German Informatics Society (*Gesellschaft für Informatik*) and are divided into the same content-related competence areas – algorithms, languages and automata, information and data, computer systems, and computer science, people and society.

## School types

| School type (*Schulform*) | Subjects with computer science | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Gymnasium (academic secondary school leading to the Abitur) | Digital World (*Digitale Welt*) (5, 6; all-day offer), from 2027/28 *AI and Digital World* (*KI und Digitale Welt*) (5, 6), computer science as elective classes (*Wahlunterricht*) (from 7), computer science in the upper secondary level (introductory phase E, Q1–Q4) | [Gymnasium](/en/teaching/germany/hesse/gymnasium/overview) |

Hauptschule (lower secondary school), Realschule (intermediate secondary school), Gesamtschulen (comprehensive schools) and the vocational Gymnasium (*berufliches Gymnasium*, among others with *Praktische Informatik* – practical computer science – and *Technische Informatik* – computer engineering) will be added.

## Ongoing transition: "Digital World" becomes "AI and Digital World"

Hesse is currently building a continuous path of computer science education (as of November 2026):

| School year | What changes |
|---|---|
| 2026/27 | The previously piloted subject **Digital World** (*Digitale Welt*) continues as a voluntary **all-day offer** (*Angebot im Ganztag*) in lower secondary for all interested schools. |
| 2027/28 | New **compulsory subject "AI and Digital World"** (*KI und Digitale Welt*) in grade 5 (one lesson per week or in blocks); in addition a compulsory **media driving license** (*Medienführerschein*) in grade 4 of primary school. |
| 2028/29 | "AI and Digital World" also in grade 6. The core curricula are to be finished by then. |
| 2029/30 | "AI and Digital World" **with a focus on computer science** (one lesson per week, in elective and compulsory elective classes) – mandatory at the Gymnasium in grade 9 **or** 10, at Hauptschulen and Realschulen in 7 or 8. |

There is no published core curriculum for the new subjects yet. The Gymnasium pages therefore describe the bases that currently apply and point out the changes.

## Klonk in relation to the core curricula

- **Blocks** can cover the programming part of *Digital World*, which so far has worked with block-based tools – without installation, also on tablets.
- **Java and Python** are the two language variants in which the Hessian state-wide Abitur exam (*Landesabitur*) sets the compulsory task on algorithmics and object-oriented modeling. Klonk supports both with the same character API.
- Microcontrollers (Calliope, micro:bit), HTML, databases and networks are part of the core curricula but are not covered by Klonk; the usual tools remain in place for those.

## For school leadership and administration

With the compulsory subject *AI and Digital World* from 2027/28 (grade 5) and 2028/29 (grade 6), and the mandatory enrolment from 2029/30, programming fundamentals are gradually becoming binding for all students in Hesse. Klonk can be used consistently from blocks in the lower grades through to Java and Python in the state-wide Abitur, and runs in the browser without installation. Information on operation, data protection, sign-in and licensing is provided on the page [For schools and school administration](/en/teaching/schools).

For the introduction to programming in *Digital World* and in elective computer science, [learning modules](/en/teaching/modules/overview) with automatically checked tasks are also available; progress is stored only in the students' browser. The grade pages indicate which modules fit.
