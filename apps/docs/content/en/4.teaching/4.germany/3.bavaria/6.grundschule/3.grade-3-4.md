**Subject:** Mathematics · **Applies to:** all students · [Full curriculum text](https://www.lehrplanplus.bayern.de/fachlehrplan/grundschule/3/mathematik)

There is no subject Computer Science in grades 3 and 4 either. In the Mathematics learning area *Space and Shape*, however, children are to **draw building plans for cube buildings and build from a model**, and describe paths. The brick stacks in Klonk correspond to such cube buildings: a building plan (top view, with the number of bricks in each cell) can be turned into a sequence of commands for the character and checked from all sides in the 3D view.

## 2.1 Orienting oneself in space

**Competence expectations (summary):** draw sketches and site plans and use them for orientation · relate two- and three-dimensional representations, build from a model and draw simple building plans for cube buildings · operate with solids by acting and in the imagination and describe procedures.

### Lesson sequence

| Step | Goal | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reading a building plan | Build a building from wooden cubes according to a plan (top view with numbers) |
| 2 | Building plan as a sequence of commands | The character builds the same building in Klonk: in each cell as many `placeBrick` as the number in the plan |
| 3 | Writing it shorter | Three identical commands in a row ⇒ `repeat 3 times` – the first loop |
| 4 | Checking | Rotate the camera: do the views from the front, the side and above match the plan? |
| 5 | Reversal | The teacher shows a finished building in Klonk; the children draw the building plan |

```klonk
// Staircase from the building plan  1 | 2 | 3
placeBrick
turnRight
step
turnLeft
placeBrick
placeBrick
turnRight
step
turnLeft
repeat 3 times
  placeBrick
end
```

### Planning notes

- The character always places bricks on the cell **in front of it**. To reach the next cell, it moves sideways – unfamiliar for children at first, so it is worth practising beforehand with a game piece on squared paper.
- With the [world editor](/en/world/editor), the teacher prepares a small world (about 5 × 5 cells) in which the character is already in the right position.
- The competence expectation calls for **relating** building plan and building – the computer does not replace building with real cubes, but is an additional representation for checking.

## Site plans and paths

In the same learning area, children draw site plans and describe paths. In Klonk, a simple site plan (schoolyard, classroom) can be rebuilt with bricks as obstacles and marks as goals; the children then describe the character's path as a sequence of commands. Local and General Studies (learning area 5.1 *Perceiving spaces and orienting oneself*) follows on with maps and orientation aids, but Klonk has no role of its own there.

## Further possibilities

- **Patterns (2.4, 1.2):** continue colour patterns with `placeBrick(red)`, `placeBrick(blue)`; with `repeat`, children recognise that a pattern consists of a repeating unit.
- **Project day or after-school club:** for interested children, the first [learning module](/en/teaching/modules/overview) **First steps: instructions** can be worked through together. The task texts are written for secondary school; the teacher should read them aloud.
