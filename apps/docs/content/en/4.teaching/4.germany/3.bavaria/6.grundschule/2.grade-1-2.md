There is no subject Computer Science in the Grundschule. The closest connection is to the Mathematics learning area *Space and Shape* (*Raum und Form*): children are to **describe the course of paths** and **follow paths according to instructions** – by acting them out and in their imagination. That is exactly what a character does in Klonk.

## 2.1 Orienting oneself in space

**Competence expectations (summary):** use positional terms (*left/right, in front of/behind, next to, between*) accurately · describe paths in the surroundings comprehensibly and follow them according to instructions · take on different perspectives.

### Lesson sequence

| Step | Goal | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Positional terms | Without a computer: one child is the robot, the others say "one step forward", "turn left". Important: *left* and *right* from the robot's point of view! |
| 2 | Path according to instructions | Lay out arrow cards on the floor and walk them; then lay the same sequence as blocks in Klonk |
| 3 | Describing a path | A prepared world contains a goal (mark). The children first describe orally, then with blocks, how the character gets there. |
| 4 | Perspective | Rotate the camera in Klonk: where is "left" for the character when it is facing me? |

```klonk
// To the yellow mark: two steps, turn left, one step
step
step
turnLeft
step
```

In German classes the same program reads `schritt`, `schritt`, `linksDrehen`, `schritt`.

### Planning notes

- In grade 1 it is sufficient for the teacher to lay the blocks on the presentation screen while the class calls out the commands.
- **Left and right** are relative to the character's viewing direction. If the character faces the class, it is exactly the other way round – a good opportunity to discuss perspective.
- Mistakes are welcome: if the character walks into a wall, Klonk shows it immediately; the children count the cells again and correct the sequence.

## 2.4 Geometric patterns and 1.2 Arithmetic patterns

With `placeBrick` (coloured bricks) and `setMark` (yellow, red, blue, green), patterns appear on the ground in Klonk, for example *red – blue – red – blue*. The children continue a pattern that has been started. This is an application example; geometric patterns are primarily developed with laying material, stamps and drawings.
