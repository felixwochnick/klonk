The **Realschule** is a secondary school type for grades 5 to 10 that leads to the intermediate school-leaving certificate (*Mittlerer Schulabschluss*). Computer science is taught there in the subject **Information Technology** (*Informationstechnologie*, IT). Algorithms and programming are compulsory for all students: regardless of the elective subject group (*Wahlpflichtfächergruppe*), the module **1.2 Introduction to Programming** (*Einführung in die Programmierung*) and later the module **2.6.1 Modelling and Coding Algorithms** (*Modellieren und Codieren von Algorithmen*) must be taught. Klonk can be used in these two compulsory modules and in some elective modules; it is not intended for the remaining modules.

## Modules instead of grades

The IT subject curriculum (*Fachlehrplan*) is **modular**; each module comprises about 14 lessons. There is no fixed assignment to grades – **the school determines** in which grade each module is taught.

| Section | When | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation course** (*Anfangsunterricht*) | starts in grade 5, 6 or 7, completed by the end of grade 8 at the latest | 9 compulsory modules for everyone (1.1–1.9), certificate of competence level I |
| **Advanced course** (*Aufbauunterricht*) | afterwards; IT lessons in grade 9 are compulsory in every elective subject group | compulsory modules depending on the elective subject group + elective modules according to the school profile, certificate of competence level II |

The Realschule pages are therefore organised by **section** instead of by individual grade:

| 8–10 | 2.6.1 Modelling and Coding Algorithms | all (compulsory) | ★★★ | [Grades 8–10](/en/teaching/germany/bavaria/realschule/grade-8-10) |

The grades in the table should be understood as a framework: schools that start IT early can begin the advanced course before grade 8.

## Elective subject groups

From grade 7, students choose an **elective subject group** (*Wahlpflichtfächergruppe*). It determines the total amount of IT teaching and the compulsory modules of the advanced course. The distribution of weekly lessons across the grades is flexible.

| Group | Focus | IT in total (weekly lessons, grades 5–10) | Focus according to the timetable (*Stundentafel*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | mathematics, science and technology | 11 | technical drawing/CAD (TZ/CAD) or computer science |
| II | economics | 8 | business administration/accounting (*Betriebswirtschaftslehre/Rechnungswesen*, BwR) |
| IIIa | foreign languages (French) | 7 | business administration/accounting |
| IIIb | compulsory elective art, crafts, nutrition and health, or social studies | 9 | TZ/CAD or computer science or BwR |

**Group I** offers the most time for programming: with the computer science focus, the elective modules 2.6.2 and 2.7 fit into the profile. Module **2.6.1**, however, is compulsory for everyone in groups II and IIIa as well.

## Progression with Klonk

Klonk can be used with the same task world across both sections; the programming language changes while the tasks remain comparable:

1. **Foundation course (module 1.2):** first algorithms with [blocks](/en/klonk-script/blocks) and [Klonk-Script](/en/klonk-script/introduction) – controlling a character, querying sensors, using variables.
2. **Advanced course (module 2.6.1):** the same tasks in a text-based language such as [Python](/en/klonk-script/python) or [Java](/en/klonk-script/java) – with functions, data types, lists and the debugger.
3. **Elective modules:** custom classes in Java (2.6.2), planning and simulating robot behaviour (2.7.2), a learning game as an IT project (2.8.5).

## Voluntary IT final examination

Realschulen can take part in a **voluntary IT final examination** (*freiwillige IT-Abschlussprüfung*). According to the ISB information letter 2024, students work on four out of six centrally set examination modules; one of them is **Modelling and Coding Algorithms**. Klonk can be used as a supplementary tool to prepare for this part of the examination.
