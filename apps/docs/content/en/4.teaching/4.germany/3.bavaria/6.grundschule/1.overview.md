The Bavarian **Grundschule** (primary school, grades 1–4) has **no subject Computer Science**. The LehrplanPLUS Grundschule contains no learning area on algorithms or programming in any subject; digital education is anchored across subjects as the cross-curricular goal *Media Education/Digital Education* (*Medienbildung/Digitale Bildung*). Using Klonk is therefore **not required**, but it can be linked to individual competence expectations of other subjects – above all in Mathematics.

The competence expectations of the Grundschule are formulated for the end of grades 2 and 4. The curriculum is designed for about 26 teaching weeks; the remaining time is available to schools for their own priorities – for example a short project "Programming a robot" as part of the school's media concept.

## Recommendation

In primary school, **acting without a computer** comes first: children play a robot, give each other commands, lay out paths with arrow cards. Klonk can follow on once the children can reliably formulate sequences of commands:

- Use **[blocks](/en/klonk-script/blocks) only** – no typing, no spelling hurdles. The blocks show the commands as words, so the children need to be able to read; in grade 1, working together on the presentation screen is recommended.
- **Few commands:** `schritt`, `linksDrehen`, `rechtsDrehen`, `hinlegen`, `markieren` (German Klonk-Script; in English `step`, `turnLeft`, `turnRight`, `placeBrick`, `setMark`). Loops (`wiederhole 3 mal`) only in grades 3/4 and only as a "shortcut" for identical commands.
- **Short units** with prepared worlds from the [world editor](/en/world/editor); the teacher distributes them as a file or via the Klonk Cloud.
- The **speed slider** and **single step** make each command visible on its own.

For getting started there are dedicated [learning modules](/en/teaching/modules/overview) with short task texts and automatic checking: **E1 Finding the way**, **E2 Building from a plan** and **E3 Patterns and colours**. All worlds are 3 × 3 cells.

## Hands-on: the world from the 3D printer

The worlds of the introductory modules can be [printed](/en/teaching/modules/printing): base plates with 3 × 3 cells that connect to each other, plus bricks, marks, blocks and a character that plugs on. All parts are compatible with common interlocking bricks. Children can first lay out and act out a task on the table and then solve it on the computer – from acting through pictures to the program.

## Transition to secondary school

Klonk is anchored in the curriculum at secondary schools – at the [Mittelschule](/en/teaching/germany/bavaria/mittelschule/overview) and at [special schools](/en/teaching/germany/bavaria/foerderschule/overview) as early as grade 5 in the compulsory subject Computer Science. Children who have gained first experience with the character in primary school will find the same environment there.
