In Lower Saxony, the **core curricula** (*Kerncurricula*, KC) issued by the Lower Saxony Ministry of Education (*Niedersächsisches Kultusministerium*) define which competences are to be acquired on a binding basis. They are published in the database [cuvo.nibis.de](https://cuvo.nibis.de) (“curricular requirements”, *Curriculare Vorgaben*) on the Lower Saxony education server. Each school determines how the competences are distributed across the grades in its **school-specific work plan** (*schuleigener Arbeitsplan*).

There are two core curricula for computer science:

| Core curriculum | Applies to | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| [Core curriculum for computer science, lower secondary, grades 5–10](https://cuvo.nibis.de/index.php?p=download&upload=185) (*Kerncurriculum Informatik für die Schulformen des Sekundarbereichs I, Schuljahrgänge 5–10*, 2014) | Compulsory subject computer science (9, 10), elective and profile courses – in all lower secondary school types | 4 learning fields (*Lernfelder*) with modules, each module with competences at the levels *basic* (*Basis*), *deepening* (*Vertiefung*), *supplement* (*Ergänzung*) |
| [Core curriculum for computer science, upper secondary](https://cuvo.nibis.de/index.php?p=download&upload=173) (*Kerncurriculum Informatik für die gymnasiale Oberstufe*, 2017, binding since 1 August 2018) | Introductory phase (*Einführungsphase*, 11) and qualification phase (*Qualifikationsphase*, 12, 13) at the Gymnasium, the comprehensive school (*Gesamtschule*) and the *Kolleg* | 3 learning fields with modules, columns *introductory phase*, *qualification phase*, *eA extension* |

## Computer science as a compulsory subject

Since the **2023/24** school year, **computer science** (*Informatik*) has been a **compulsory subject** in lower secondary education at all general-education school types: first in grade 10, and since **2024/25** also in grade 9 – each with **one lesson per week**. It is based on the 2014 core curriculum for lower secondary; a new core curriculum has not yet been published in cuvo (as of 2 November 2026).

Klonk can be assigned above all to the learning field **“Algorithmic problem solving”** (*Algorithmisches Problemlösen*). There, the core curriculum provides for implementing algorithms first in a graphical and later also in a text-based programming language; Klonk offers both in one environment.

## For school leadership and administration

With computer science compulsory in grades 9 and 10, all general-education lower secondary schools need a programming environment that works within one lesson per week and can be anchored in the school-specific work plan. Klonk runs in the browser without installation and can be used consistently across the grades – from blocks to a text-based language in upper secondary. Information on operation, data protection, sign-in and licensing is provided on the page [For schools and school administration](/en/teaching/schools).

For algorithmic problem solving in grades 9 and 10, [learning modules](/en/teaching/modules/overview) with automatically checked tasks are also available; progress is stored only in the students' browser. The grade pages indicate which modules fit.
